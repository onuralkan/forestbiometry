## Diameter Distribution Lab 2.2.0

- **Optional methods** (not selected by default): L-moments (Weibull, gamma); general quantile matching with your
  probabilities and weights; minimum distance by KS, Cramér–von Mises or Anderson–Darling (normal, gamma, beta). They
  do not maximise the likelihood and are compared only descriptively; a row fitted by the criterion a table is sorted
  by is marked †.
- **Johnson SB all-parameter recovery** after Fonseca, Marques and Parresol (2009) from the median, mean, Dq and the
  basal-area-weighted mean diameter; a separate method next to Parresol (2003). Symmetric or weakly determined inputs
  give no single answer.
- **Optional families:** generalized gamma (positive c; maximum likelihood by the profile in c; gamma and Weibull as
  special cases) and Gumbel, maximum form (maximum likelihood and moments; the probability of a negative diameter F(0)
  is reported). Measurement thresholds and grouped data are supported for both. The comparison table notes that more
  parameters do not make a better biological model.
- **Empirical basal area diameter distribution** (descriptive, Kangas and Maltamo 2000): F_G next to F_N, diameters at
  the 0–100 % basal area percentiles and dgM, class basal area and its conversion to numbers of trees. No family is
  fitted to basal-area-weighted data.
- Burr XII, Dagum and two-component mixtures, planned in the specification, are left out of scope.

## Stem Section Volume 1.0.0-beta.2 (new)

- Single-tree stem volume from measured section diameters by Huber, Smalian, Newton or Hohenadl. You choose the data
  layout (section ends, middles, end–middle–end, or 0.1H…0.9H); methods that do not fit the layout are not offered.
- Stump and top piece apart (top piece: cone by default, paraboloid or neiloid), over and under bark, form factor,
  merchantable volume to a top diameter, per-hectare totals; trees that cannot be computed are counted, never filled in.
- Hohenadl after Heger (1965), with the true form quotients and λ0.9.
- A data quality report and the differences between methods (no method is called correct).

## Site Index 1.0.0-beta.3 (new)

- Site index at a base age from top height and age with Chapman–Richards (anamorphic, polymorphic) and Schumacher
  difference equations. Equations from Hernández-Cuevas et al. (2018).
- No built-in species or regional coefficients: you enter a coefficient set with its source, or fit one to your own stem
  analyses or repeated measurements (with a simulated sample to try).
- The age type is chosen explicitly and never converted. User-defined site classes, curve family, confidence intervals
  by a series-level bootstrap or by the delta method with the source's covariance matrix.
- Top height from approved tree data with the same definition as Plot & Stand Metrics.

## Diameter Distribution Lab 2.1.0: stand models (part of 2.2.0)

- A new "Stand model" workflow predicts the diameter distribution from the number of trees, stand height and age. The
  model is trained on many plots, using Poudel and Cao's (2013) form y = exp(β₁ + β₂RS + β₃lnN + β₄lnH + β₅/A) + ε.
- Approaches:
  - PPM: the Weibull b and c are predicted directly; the location is a = 0 or a = 0.5 × the predicted Dmin.
  - PRM: the targets of Weibull M2–M9 or of the Johnson SB Parresol recovery are predicted, and the parameters are
    recovered from them.
- Fitting methods:
  - NLS, on the original scale.
  - Iterated SUR, with the residual covariance between equations.
  - log-OLS, with the back-transformation stated.
  - CDFR and modified CDFR: coefficients shared by all plots; the source's weights; true class upper bounds; the last
    class is left out of the objective only.
- Validation is at plot level: all periods of a plot stay in one fold, and each held-out unit gets its −ln L and KS.
- Predictions:
  - are made only with a model trained in the tool or imported from a model file;
  - are checked for consistency, with no clipping;
  - are flagged when they extrapolate beyond the training data.
- Models can be exported and imported as a JSON file holding the coefficients, units, training scope and validation.
- The tool ships a simulated sample stand data set.

## User guide

`kilavuz.php` (Turkish and English) describes, for every tool: what it does and does not do, the data it needs, the
steps, the results, the downloads and common situations. Each tool's header links to its section.

## Diameter Distribution Lab 2.0.1 (interface)

- **Settings:**
  - methods appear in a grid;
  - formulas and notes are behind a "Details" disclosure for each family;
  - choices, inputs and errors stay visible;
  - a sticky action bar shows how many methods are selected.
- **Results:**
  - parameters and model comparison are merged into one Results tab: one sortable table of all fits, grouped by
    observation process;
  - secondary criteria are behind an "all criteria" switch;
  - notes are in a single list;
  - the full per-family tables are in a disclosure;
  - there are four tabs instead of five.
- **Recovery from summaries:**
  - a guide table shows which methods can be used with the summaries at hand;
  - the three method groups are on separate tabs;
  - the mean, variance, Dq, n and variance divisor are typed once and shared between the groups.
- **Header:**
  - the tool version is shown in the page header of every tool;
  - the release-notes banner is removed;
  - the introduction is shorter.
- **Citations:** the Cite dialog, the README and `CITATION.cff` carry the Zenodo concept DOI 10.5281/zenodo.24135603.
